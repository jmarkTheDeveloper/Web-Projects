Fire Emergency Protocol

1

Activate Alarm & Call Emergency Services

Pull the nearest fire alarm immediately. Dial 911 or the local Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) once you are safe.

2

Stay Low to the Ground

Toxic smoke rises to the ceiling. Crawl on your hands and knees to the nearest exit to keep your head in the safe breathing zone.

3

Test Doors Before Opening

Use the back of your hand to feel the door, the knob, and the crack between the door and frame. If hot, keep the door closed and use a secondary escape route.

4

Stop, Drop, and Roll

If your clothing catches fire, do not run. Stop immediately, drop to the floor, cover your face with your hands, and roll over continuously until the flames are out.

🌍

Earthquake Survival (Duck, Cover, Hold)

1

Drop, Cover, and Hold On

Immediately drop to your hands and knees. Take cover under a sturdy desk or table to protect your head and neck. Hold on to your shelter so it doesn't bounce away.

2

Stay Away from Hazards

Move away from glass, windows, outside doors, and heavy furniture or fixtures that could fall. Do not use elevators under any circumstances.

3

If Outdoors, Find a Clear Spot

If you are outside, move to an open area away from buildings, streetlights, and utility wires. Drop to the ground and stay there until the shaking stops.

🌊

Flooding Evacuation

1

Evacuate to Higher Ground

Move immediately to higher elevation if a flash flood warning is issued. Do not wait for instructions to move.

2

Turn Around, Don't Drown

Never walk, swim, or drive through floodwaters. Just 6 inches of moving water can knock you down, and 12 inches can sweep a car away.

3

Disconnect Electrical Utilities

If instructed, or if water begins entering your home, turn off the main electrical breaker. Never touch electrical equipment if you are wet or standing in water.

🎒

72-Hour Emergency Go-Bag

Your "Go-Bag" should contain enough essential items to sustain you for at least 72 hours. Keep it easily accessible near your exit.

✔

Water and Non-Perishable Food

At least 1 gallon of water per person per day. Include easy-to-prepare, non-perishable foods like canned goods, energy bars, and MREs.

✔

Tools & Electronics

A battery-powered or hand-crank radio, flashlight with extra batteries, whistle to signal for help, multi-tool, and a power bank with charging cables.

✔

Personal Items & Documents

Change of sturdy clothes, personal hygiene items, cash in small bills, and copies of important documents (IDs, insurance) sealed in a waterproof bag.

⚕️

First-Aid & Medical Kit

A properly stocked first-aid kit is crucial for treating minor injuries and managing health during a crisis.

+

Wound Care Essentials

Assorted adhesive bandages, sterile gauze pads, medical tape, antiseptic wipes, antibiotic ointment, and a CPR breathing barrier.

+

Medications

A 7-day supply of any prescription medications. Over-the-counter pain relievers (ibuprofen/acetaminophen), antacids, and anti-diarrhea medication.

+

Medical Tools

Tweezers (for splinters), trauma shears/scissors, non-latex gloves, digital thermometer, and an instant cold compress.
